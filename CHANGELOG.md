# Changelog

All notable changes to Diony AI are documented here.

## Unreleased

### Added
- **One Open and one Build launcher per desk.** `Open Diony AI.command` / `Open Diony AI.bat` open the built app when the folder holds one (runtime and ffmpeg inside; the window can close at once) and otherwise run the desk from source and say so. `Build Diony AI.bat` and `scripts/release/build-local-windows.ps1` are the Windows twins of the Mac local build: payload staged and hashed, PyInstaller runtime, the pinned ffmpeg in the runtime folder, a non-publishable development report, a stable `dist\desktop\<version>\development\Diony AI\` junction, unchanged payloads skipped. The development report knows both app layouts and carries the platform and its recipe (`macos-development-v4`, `windows-development-v1`). The signed release builders stage the pinned ffmpeg too, joined into Universal 2 binaries on the Mac. `docs/product-listing.md` holds the storefront copy.
- **ffmpeg comes with Diony.** The desktop build packages a pinned static ffmpeg + ffprobe beside the app (`ffmpeg_lock.json`: URL and SHA-256 per file; 9.0.1 on macOS, 8.1.2 on Windows), and the desk verifies them on every open before any service starts — when a bundle lacks them it installs the same pinned release into the data folder, verified, with no package manager, terminal, or password, and exports `DIONY_FFMPEG` / `DIONY_FFPROBE` to every service. Studio's setup instructions shrink to the keys file on both desks, with one status line for ffmpeg; the missing-ffmpeg message in the Director and Producer now says to reopen the app. `python -m diony_desk.media fetch <platform> <dir>` stages the release for a build. NOTICE carries the FFmpeg (GPL) section.

- **Mac and Windows, one product.** Studio's opening carries a **This computer** toggle (Mac | Windows), preset to the desk it detected (`detected_platform()`), persisted at `{vault}/.cluster/platform.json` (`POST /api/opening/platform`). Every word the product says that differs between desks comes from one place, `diony_contracts.cluster.platform_hints()`: the keys path, the setup commands (Terminal or PowerShell), the ffmpeg install line (`brew install ffmpeg` or `winget install Gyan.FFmpeg`), the launcher name, the one-time names prompt (password or User Account Control), the data directory. The keys explainer shows the chosen desk's block; the Director's and the Gallery's missing-ffmpeg messages read the same hint instead of a Mac-only `brew`. ffmpeg discovery adds the Windows homes (winget links, Chocolatey, scoop, Program Files, `C:\ffmpeg\bin`, with `.exe`). The runtime was already per-desk (hosts file, privilege prompt, Python discovery, data directory); this makes what the user reads match.
- **The Google Flow camera thinks and speaks on Google.** A Gemini API key (`GEMINI_API_KEY`, Google AI Studio) gives the Google Flow camera its own house: `diony_director.brain` resolves the writer by camera — Gemini 2.5 Pro through Gemini's OpenAI-compatible endpoint on Google Flow, Grok 4.6 on Grok Imagine — with the desk's **Brain** pill (`{vault}/.cluster/brain.json`, `GET/POST /api/director/brain`) able to name either house when both keys exist, an explicit `DIONY_DIRECTOR_BRAIN_URL` still overriding everything, and a missing house key borrowing the other with a stated reason (the header's brain chip names the house and the model). The narrator follows the same rule: a new **Gemini speech** backend (`generateContent` with `responseModalities: AUDIO`, prebuilt voices offered as `Gemini · Kore` and company, 24 kHz PCM wrapped to WAV, the words verbatim with no tag or style text read aloud) is the Google Flow camera's narrator, the Grok voice API stays Grok Imagine's, and the Voice pill offers every roster the desk holds a key for — a voice id names its house, so a Flow film may keep a Grok narrator and a Grok film a Gemini one. Studio's opening reports the Gemini key, says under the Google Flow card whether its script and narrator are Google or borrowed, and explains the Gemini key beside the others; `diony-director-check` reports the key and `--live` probes the Gemini model list. A desk holding only the Google keys runs the whole path on Google with nothing missing. Director stamp p55.

### Added

- **Blueprint 33 — long takes, and a cast dock that never needs scrolling.** Draft Script split one script line into one clip, so a 16–20s scene became four or five 4–6s clips on engines that shoot 15s (Grok) and 10s (omni) in one generation — five identity rolls and five chain joins where one or two would do. The splitter now dissects a scene into beats and PACKS them into takes: the fewest the route's longest legal clip allows (a quarter of pace tolerance, so 18s of beats is one 15s take on Grok), as even as the beats permit (a balanced partition — 30s is two 15s takes, 22s is 11s + 11s, never 15s + 3s), one spoken line per take always (every consumer reads one excerpt per clip), each lane's cap held (a take with a line is capped by the omni grid under Auto/Google Flow), each take paced to its scene's even share and recorded on `ClipUnit.natural_s` before the grid rounds it up. A take's camera is every authored shot in it as a progression; a line rides with its setup beat and its reaction in the same frame. `AudioPacket.line_at_s` says where the words begin inside the take: the post narrator's cue and the subtitle land there, and the assembly's dead-air trim follows the whole take, never the word count alone. The screenwriter is told the route's take length (`take_brief`) so it writes continuous motion for long takes; the draft and lock replies name it (`takes of up to 15s (10s when a line is spoken in it)`). And approving a cast no longer means scrolling the thread for the Production card: while a locked film has a face awaiting approval, the console carries a **cast dock** — one row per character (anchor still, name, the measured sheet's verdict in words, Approve), **Approve all** (`POST …/bible/approve_all`, landed faces only, a drawing sheet named for the next press), and **Cast ▸** to the BIBLES section — visible even while the run strip folds the console, one quiet line until a sheet draws or lands (`sheet_state` on the board, read from the sheet ops), gone once every face is approved; SHOTS rows name each take's beat count. And a script locked and priced but never rolled is unfinished business: the room resumes it (`resumable` rows carry a `stage`, `priced` behind any `running` room) from `GET …/sessions/{sid}/lock` — the read-only draft card, the priced cards with their batch bar, the Production card and the dock — instead of opening a fresh room over a package on disk; ＋ NEW SESSION clears the dock with the room. - **Retry redraws the opening frame and resumes the run.** 2026-09-06, clip 6/30 of a chained one-shot: Google Flow refused the clip (prominent-people filter) and every Retry re-rolled it from the SAME style frame — the still carrying the recognizable face — into the same refusal, four times; and each Retry re-armed only that clip, so the later links (dropped from the batch when their predecessor failed) needed Run & Assemble All pressed again after every retake. - **A real person is described, never named — the likeness guard.** 2026-09-05: the idea named nobody; the reference still was a truck poster; the brain, which sees the still, recognized the actor and wrote his name into the master prompt and all 26 clip prompts (`Create a video of <actor> locked in a full split…`, `<actor>'s face`) — the exact prompt Google Flow's prominent-people filter refuses before render, and no visual instruction anyway. `diony_director.likeness` closes it in three layers (Blueprint 27 §3a). The brain is told: both the master template and the screenwriter carry a LIKENESS LAW — never write a real, identifiable person's name, nickname, or identifying title from the idea or a still, describe them as a character, and confess who was recognized in a new `real_people` key. - **Blueprint 31 — Production Style: Narrated, Dialogue, Mix.** The Director console gained a **Style** pill beside Voice. **Narrated** is the default and is byte-identical to the platform before this change: one post narrator records every line against the cut (Blueprint 28), every location track speechless by law. **Dialogue** puts the script lines INTO each clip's generation prompt — quoted verbatim, with the instruction that the character speaks them aloud on camera, lips in sync, and that nothing else is spoken on the track — so the provider's native audio is the voice; no post narrator runs. **Mix** uses both instruments where they belong: on-camera dialogue is voiced by the character in the clip, voice-over lines and one-shot narration are recorded in post, and the cut balances and mutes from the script. The style resolves at the split (and at one-shot draft time) into a per-clip `voicing` on the contracts (`AudioPacket.voicing`, `DirectorOp.voicing`, `ProductionPackage.production_style`) — the compiler (all three dialects), review, the speech guard, the cue sheet, the mix, and the EDL read the clip's own voicing, never the session's style, so footage already shot is never reinterpreted; the style is refused (409) once the script locks. Review measures a spoken clip the other way round: the track is transcribed once and scored against the authored line — a new `line` family fails when the character stayed mute or said other words (naming what was heard and missed), passes on-script, stays honestly `unmeasured` without a whisper model — and plans a `fix-line` retake (the line, clearly, once, and nothing else) instead of Blueprint 28's remove-all-speech fix. At the cut the speech guard keeps every spoken track (never muted), RESCUES a spoken clip whose track measures no talking voice by having the narrator record that line on its mark, BALANCES every kept spoken track to the narrator's −15 LUFS whenever the cut has a narrator rail (so on-camera dialogue and narration sit at one level), keeps Blueprint 28's mutes for post clips, and never tail-trims a spoken take to the word-count arithmetic; every decision is a receipt (`speech_guard.kept/rescued/balanced/muted`, a `style` block on the assemble response) and the room reads them back. A Dialogue film assembles with no narrator backend on the host. The master-prompt brain contract follows the voicing (under generated voicing the system appends the spoken performance; the default contract is unchanged). Also fixed on the way: the brain-off template derivation landed a master with empty narration over the seeded authored lines of a one-shot — the authored words now stand whenever the master carries none. `GET /api/director/styles`, `POST …/sessions/{sid}/style`, `style` on message/produce bodies and in `…/settings`, persisted in state.json. UI stamp p37.

- **Flow is never hit in a burst.** The submitter, the chain and master workers, every advance tick, and a reconnect push all spoke to useapi.net's Google Flow endpoints independently, so a wave, a bible print, and a polling tick could land requests back-to-back or on top of each other — the shape that earns `PUBLIC_ERROR_USER_REQUESTS_THROTTLED` and a 30-minute hold. A failed request counts toward the gap — it still touched the API. Media downloads go to Google's storage, not the Flow API, and are not paced.
